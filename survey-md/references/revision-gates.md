# Revision Gates For Existing Survey Drafts

Use these gates whenever the task begins from a manuscript draft. Audit before rewriting sentences. Apply the gates to the whole manuscript, not only to the passages the user happened to flag.

## Audit Artifact

Create or update `11-revision-audit.md` with one row per failure:

| Location | Gate | Failure | Required scholarly content | Evidence needed | Repair action | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use `blocking`, `resolved`, or `accepted-boundary` for status. `Accepted-boundary` requires an explicit reason and appropriately limited manuscript wording. Keep audit statuses, checklist prompts, and template field names in the audit artifact. A working table, card, or diagram may enter the manuscript only when it has been rewritten as a scholarly table or figure that conveys a domain relationship and supports a prose claim.

## Repair Contract

A blocking gate creates a repair obligation. For every blocking row:

1. State what the reader must understand that the current text does not provide.
1. Identify which verified evidence can support that explanation and what evidence is still missing.
1. Choose the least destructive repair that restores the argument: define, explain, connect, narrow, relocate, reorder, merge, split, or rewrite.
1. Re-run the failed gate on the repaired passage and record why it now passes.

Do not satisfy a gate by deleting difficult material when the review question, promised scope, or central thesis still requires it. Delete or demote material only when the audit establishes that it is redundant, out of scope, unsupported, or unnecessary to the argument. If evidence is missing for required content, preserve the question, mark the evidence gap, narrow the claim, and seek or request the needed source rather than silently erasing the topic.

When replacing or removing an organizing axis, redistribute its valid papers, evidence units, concepts, and unresolved questions into the repaired structure. Record an explicit disposition for every displaced unit. A taxonomy repair passes only when coverage and explanatory value are preserved or deliberately changed with a documented scope decision.

## Gate 1: Thesis And Reader Payoff

Extract the central thesis as a contestable proposition about the literature. Map each major section to the part of that thesis it establishes, qualifies, or challenges.

Block the draft when:

- the thesis only announces a topic, paper set, taxonomy, or writing choice;
- the abstract or introduction leads with defensive self-description instead of the scholarly problem and position;
- a major section does not change the reader's understanding of the thesis;
- the conclusion asserts a position that the body did not prepare.

Preserve a defensible authorial position. Revision must improve its evidence and precision, not flatten it into neutral inventory.

## Gate 2: Concept And Dependency Order

List every term that controls the thesis, taxonomy, mechanism, or evaluation. For each term, record its first conceptual use, definition location, kind, applicability condition, and distinction from its nearest neighbor.

Block the draft when:

- a term is used before the reader can identify its referent and role;
- a definition is only a translation, synonym, example, motivation, or unexplained metaphor;
- two levels such as entity, representation, operation, relation, claim, and metric are treated as interchangeable;
- definitions are circular or silently change across sections.

Define a term before or at first conceptual use. Move detail later if needed, but give the reader enough information to interpret the first claim containing it.

## Gate 3: Taxonomy Validity

Treat every taxonomy as a substantive claim, not a formatting device. For each axis, record:

- the exact classification question;
- the unit being classified;
- the membership criterion for every category;
- allowed overlap and how multi-membership is represented;
- the explanatory consequence of changing categories;
- why this axis is needed for the review thesis.

Block and repair an axis when:

- category labels name surface nouns but do not identify a stable analytical distinction;
- categories mix different conceptual levels or classify different units;
- a work fits several categories but the taxonomy presents them as exclusive;
- changing category does not change the object of study, mechanism, evidence, assumption, or scope;
- the prose needs repeated exceptions to make the categories fit;
- the axis inventories implementations without helping answer the review question.

Do not preserve a weak taxonomy merely because it already structures the draft. Compare alternative organizations and record why the retained one explains the literature better. Repair by clarifying criteria, relabeling, merging, splitting, or replacing the axis. Remove it only when it contributes nothing necessary to the thesis; otherwise rehome its valid content before the gate can pass.

## Gate 4: Explanatory Sufficiency

For every major approach, method family, theory, or empirical line, ensure the prose lets a field-aware reader answer:

1. What problem is shared with the surrounding approaches?
1. What object, relation, assumption, or mechanism does this line change?
1. How does the proposed mechanism or theoretical move work at the level needed for the review's claim?
1. What immediate result, artifact, observation, or proposition follows?
1. How does that immediate result participate in, explain, or support the broader phenomenon or outcome named by the review?
1. What evidence supports that interpretation?
1. What does the evidence not establish, and under which conditions does the conclusion hold?
1. How does this line differ from and relate to adjacent lines of work?

Block the section when the reader would need to open the cited paper merely to understand what the route is doing, or when the explanation stops at an intermediate representation, measurement, artifact, or proposition without connecting it to the broader phenomenon or outcome used to motivate the category. Repair the missing explanatory links from verified sources; if the evidence cannot support them, narrow the claim or record a source gap. Do not compensate with model names, representation names, author lists, performance numbers, or adjectives, and do not delete a required approach merely because it is difficult to explain. If the subject is an operational process, explain the necessary process. If it is theoretical or descriptive, explain the corresponding reasoning, mechanism, or inferential structure instead.

Also block paper-internal shorthand that substitutes for an explanation. Phrases such as `under a unified protocol`, `the factors are isolated`, `the modules are aligned`, `the method is end-to-end`, or `the ablation verifies` pass only when nearby prose identifies the concrete data/conditions held fixed, the component or signal changed, the outcome compared, and the inference licensed by that comparison. Apply the same test to unexplained acronyms and named modules.

## Gate 5: Synthesis And Evidence-Bound Judgment

For each paragraph, identify the field-level claim, the evidence units supporting it, the relationship among sources, the author's inference, and the boundary on that inference.

Block the paragraph when:

- it can be reordered paper by paper without changing its meaning;
- citations accumulate without contrast, consequence, or adjudication;
- criticism is detached from the comparison that makes it visible;
- the authorial judgment is stronger than the cited evidence;
- a future direction is not earned by a demonstrated unresolved boundary.

## Gate 6: Manuscript Surface

After structural gates pass, remove working-process residue. Check every heading, opening paragraph, transition, table lead-in, and conclusion.

Block the draft when:

- a heading advertises the number of axes, rejects a discarded label, or narrates the writer's organizational choice;
- the opening pages or scope statement define the survey primarily by what it is not; describe actual coverage and protocol limits affirmatively, and place necessary methodological boundaries in the method or scope discussion;
- audit statuses, checklist prompts, template field names, or notes about the writer's organizational decision appear as finished prose instead of being converted into scholarly content;
- a section begins with classification before establishing the concepts and relationships that make the classification intelligible;
- prose is formally polished but still conveys no mechanism, relationship, evidence boundary, or scholarly judgment.
- headings, topic sentences, the abstract, or the conclusion repeatedly use correction templates (`not X but Y`, `X is not equal to Y`, `not only X`, `X cannot represent Y`) where the underlying relation can be stated directly; isolated evidence-backed negation is allowed, but recurring reversal cadence is a surface and argument-design failure.
- a work's first substantive mention is a bare title/link or author ledger entry when verified institution and year metadata are available; repair it as `institution/team + linked Work(Year) + evidence role`, while leaving later mentions compact.

## Gate 7: Reader Reconstruction Test

Read each taxonomy axis and each major synthesis section without consulting the internal workspace. Write a two-to-four sentence reconstruction containing the shared problem, the distinguishing mechanism or claim, how any intermediate product connects to the broader phenomenon or outcome, the evidence, and the boundary.

Block the draft if the reconstruction requires guessing, importing unstated domain knowledge, or repeating labels without explaining their relationship. Rewrite the section, then repeat the test. Do not mark the manuscript ready until every axis and major section passes.

As a final cold-read, paraphrase each dense evidence sentence without using the cited paper's terminology. If the paraphrase cannot name who did what to which object, under what comparison, and with what consequence, the original sentence remains blocking.
